# Prose Style

Rules for anything the owner might publish or send: website copy, posts, emails, client documents, social drafts. The goal is writing that sounds like a person wrote it.

## Banned phrases (never use)

- "Here's the thing" / "But here's the thing"
- "Let me break it down" / "Let's dive in" / "Let's delve into"
- "And honestly?" / "Honestly," / "I'll be honest"
- "Let that sink in" / "Read that again"
- "This changes everything"
- "...and that's rare" / "...and that matters" (as a closing kicker)
- "The takeaway" (as a header or transition)
- "What you need to understand is" / "Let me be clear"
- "Here's the / where / why" as a sentence opener: lead with the point instead

## Banned structures

- **"It's not X. It's Y."** as a flourish. Fine when genuinely distinguishing two things; not fine as false profundity ("It's not a course. It's a movement.").
- **"Not A. Not B. But C."** The triple-beat reveal. Never.
- **Staccato fragment pileups** for drama: "A phrase. A fragment. A word." The most recognizable AI cadence.

## Cadence (use judgment)

- Not every paragraph needs a tidy punchline at the end. Vary the endings.
- Don't over-parallel. If three sentences share a shape, break one.
- Three bullets that are each a full sentence are probably a paragraph.
- Don't throat-clear ("worth noting," "the key insight here"). Just say the thing.
- Em dashes are fine; more than two in a paragraph starts to read as a tic.

## Facts first

Stick to facts the owner gave you or that you checked. Flowery or emotional language should be the owner's own words. If a claim (a number, a credential, a feature) can't be verified, ask instead of guessing.

## Before you hand it over

Re-read for rhythm. If every paragraph has the same shape, break some. Plain, slightly irregular prose reads as human.
